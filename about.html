Our story

Built on one craftsman's standard for the work.

RadioMati carries a family name into a modern engineering shop. The tools are different now; the standard remains the same.

The workshop

Mati would not return a set until it was properly repaired.

RadioMati is named after Mati, who ran a radio repair workshop and laboratory known above all for one thing: he took no shortcuts. Customers brought him sets no one else could restore, and he had the patience — and the standards — to see the repair through properly.

That reputation is the reason the company carries his name. It is not a marketing narrative — it remains the standard the company measures itself against today, on circuit boards rather than radios.

From repair to R&D

Same bench discipline, aimed at harder problems.

Then

The workshop

A one-man radio and electronics repair laboratory, built on patient diagnosis and craftsmanship — no job left at "good enough."

Since

From repair to R&D

The next generation pointed that same discipline forward — into designing hardware from scratch rather than only fixing it: power electronics, analog and control circuits, motion systems, embedded Linux.

Today

RadioMati now

A small, dedicated team led by CEO Idan Avrutzki, running R&D-to-production projects for industrial and defense customers — design, rapid prototyping, in-house SMT and through-hole production, and EMI/environmental testing, all on-site in Misgav.

What that means in practice

Three things the company does not compromise on.

01 — Accompaniment

We remain on the project, not only the drawing.

From the first specification conversation to a finished, working unit, the same team carries the project through — so responsibility for the result is never divided by a handoff.

02 — Local manufacturing

We build what we design.

Our own SMT line and assembly bench mean a prototype is never merely a drawing handed to a third party — it is a working unit, produced quickly, that we can rework the same day.

03 — Tested, not assumed

We verify before we call it complete.

EMI, environmental and integration testing are performed in-house, so a design's performance is confirmed by measurement before it ships — not assumed from calculation alone.
